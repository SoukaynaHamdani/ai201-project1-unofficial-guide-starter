# UNOFFICIAL GUIDE: AVOIDING SUBLET & RENTAL SCAMS NEAR UC BERKELEY
# (Compiled from UCPD scam warnings, the Off-Campus Housing office, and student experiences.)
#
# WHY THIS MATTERS
# Every August and January, Berkeley housing demand spikes and scammers target
# students who are desperate for a place before classes start. Facebook groups
# ("UC Berkeley Housing, Sublets & Roommates"), Craigslist, and even DMs on Reddit
# are common hunting grounds. The pressure to lock something in fast is exactly
# what scammers exploit.
#
# THE MOST COMMON RED FLAGS
# 1. They ask for money before you ever see the unit in person.
# 2. The "landlord" claims to be out of the country (job abroad, missionary trip,
#    military deployment) and cannot show you the place.
# 3. They demand payment by wire transfer, Zelle, Venmo, gift cards, or crypto,
#    any method that is hard to trace or reverse.
# 4. The rent is suspiciously cheap for the location ("$900 for a studio two
#    blocks from campus"). If it feels too good to be true, it is.
# 5. They rush you: "I have 10 other people interested, send the deposit in the
#    next hour to hold it."
# 6. The listing photos are stolen, reverse image search them; they often show up
#    on real estate sites in other cities.
# 7. They refuse a video call or in-person meeting, or only communicate by text.
#
# THE CLASSIC "I'M ABROAD" SCRIPT
# A poster says they own a unit but had to leave the country urgently. They will
# mail you the keys after you wire a deposit and first month's rent. You drive by
# the building, it looks real, so you pay. The keys never arrive, and the actual
# tenants living there have no idea their building was used in a scam.
#
# THE SUBLET / ROOMMATE VARIANT
# You are the one listing a room. Someone "moving from out of state" sends a
# cashier's check for MORE than the deposit, then asks you to refund the
# difference. The check bounces days later, you are out the money you "refunded."
# Never accept overpayment or send money back to a stranger.
#
# HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF
# - See the actual unit in person (or send a trusted friend) before paying anything.
# - Verify the landlord owns the property: Alameda County keeps public property
#   records you can check against the name on the lease.
# - Use the official Cal Rentals / Off-Campus Housing listings (och.berkeley.edu)
#   where listings are tied to verified accounts.
# - Get everything in writing and sign a real lease before money changes hands.
# - Pay by a method that is traceable and reversible; avoid wire and gift cards.
# - Never wire money or send a deposit to someone you have not met.
# - If a deal feels rushed or "off," walk away. There is always another listing.
#
# IF YOU GET SCAMMED
# - Report it to UCPD (university police) and the Berkeley Police Department.
# - File a complaint with the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
# - Warn other students by posting in the housing groups, you may save someone else.
# - Contact your bank immediately; some transfers can be stopped if caught fast.
#
# BOTTOM LINE
# A legitimate landlord will meet you, let you tour the unit, and accept normal
# payment after a signed lease. Anyone who won't do those three things is a risk.
